Career Research & Planning

Primary Career and Backup Career

The following research compares a game development career path with welding as a backup career. It includes education requirements, occupational details, strengths, recommendations, and possible gaps.

Career 1

Primary Career — Game Developer

O*NET Occupation: Video Game Designers / Software Developers

Education

Level: Bachelor’s degree. Of respondents, 55% report requiring a bachelor’s degree, while 15% require an associate degree or some college.

Job zone: Job Zone Four: Considerable Preparation Needed. The SVP range is 7.0 to less than 8.0.

Amarillo College programs: CIS — Game Design Certificate (Major Code: COSC.CERT.GAME) or Computer Information Systems. The certificate is 18 credit hours, approximately 2 semesters, while the full A.A.S. is 60 credit hours, approximately 4 semesters.

Curriculum highlights: Design and Creation of Games (GAME 1306), Game Engine (GAME 2319), Project Development I (GAME 2332), Programming Logic and Design (ITSE 1329), Computer Programming (ITSE 1302), Object-Oriented Programming (ITSE 2321), Digital Imaging I (ARTC 1302), Intro to Computer Graphics (ARTC 1325), and Basic Animation (ARTV 1303).

Career Requirements & Recommendations

Industry standards lean heavily on a public technical portfolio or playable game demos rather than credentials alone. Studios evaluate version-controlled source code, gameplay loops, and design documents.

  • Transfer credits from Amarillo College toward a four-year Bachelor of Science in Computer Science, Game Design, or Software Engineering.
  • Maintain a public GitHub repository demonstrating game mechanics.
  • Participate in game jams.
  • Master modern production engines such as Unreal Engine 5 and Godot 4.

Matches with YouScience

  • Spatial Visualization: Critical for level layout, 3D world building, camera mathematics, and spatial physics.
  • Numerical & Sequential Reasoning: Directly supports scripting game logic, conditional branching, state machines, math vectors, and algorithmic problem-solving.
  • Artistic & Conventional Interests: Supports creative world aesthetics, UI layout, and narrative design while also using asset hierarchies, version control, and structured documentation.
  • Introvert Style: Well-suited to long, uninterrupted stretches of code development, level construction, and debugging.

Possible Gaps

  • Generalist approach vs. AAA specialization: Large commercial studios often recruit narrow specialists such as rendering engineers or network specialists. A generalist works best in indie development, technical design, or small teams.
  • Communication demands: Solo development can favor introversion, but professional studio environments require design reviews, sprint planning, and cross-disciplinary standups with animators, producers, and QA testers.

Career 2

Backup Career — Welder

O*NET Occupation: Welders, Cutters, Solderers, and Brazers

Education

Level: High school diploma or equivalent, followed by postsecondary certificate or technical vocational training.

Job zone: Job Zone Two: Some Preparation Needed. The SVP range is 4.0 to less than 6.0.

Amarillo College programs: Welding Technology Basic Certificate (WELD.CERT.BASIC), Intermediate Certificate, Advanced Certificate (WLDG.CERT.ADV), or A.A.S.

Duration: The basic certificate is 39 credit hours, approximately 2 semesters; the advanced certificate is approximately 48 credit hours, approximately 3 semesters; and the full A.A.S. is 60 credit hours, approximately 4 semesters.

Curriculum highlights: Shielded Metal Arc Welding I and II (WLDG 1375, WLDG 1376), Gas Metal Arc Welding or MIG (WLDG 1377), Gas Tungsten Arc Welding or TIG (WLDG 1378), Layout and Fabrication I and II (WLDG 1372, WLDG 2372), Thermal Cutting I (WLDG 1373), Bench Work and Layout (MCHN 1332), Machine Shop Mathematics (MCHN 1343), and practical AWS D1.1 structural welding qualification testing.

Career Requirements & Recommendations

Industry certifications commonly issued through the American Welding Society (AWS) or ASME, particularly structural AWS D1.1 and pressure-pipe ASME Section IX test plates.

  • Complete an OSHA 10-Hour or 30-Hour General Industry or Construction Safety card.
  • Practice practical structural and pipe welding test bends.
  • Gain hands-on fabrication experience through industrial internships or local machine shops.

Matches with YouScience

  • Realistic Interest: Directly aligns with hands-on tool operation, material manipulation, and physical fabrication.
  • Conventional Interest: Aligns with stringent weld codes, dimensional tolerance measurements, and welding procedure specifications (WPS).
  • Spatial Visualization: Translates two-dimensional blueprint schematics and cut lists into multi-axis 3D metal assemblies and weld joints.
  • Numerical & Sequential Reasoning: Supports joint geometry, heat input calculations, feed speeds, and sequential weld passes to manage heat distortion.
  • Introvert Style: Welding is focused, solitary bench and booth work behind the hood.

Possible Gaps

  • Artistic Interest Expression: Standard production welding offers limited creative outlet compared with digital media, unless directed toward custom fabrication, ornamental ironwork, or automotive prototyping.
  • Work Approach: Welding often rewards repetitive procedural mastery in a single process rather than broad generalist multitasking. General fabrication and repair shops can still provide varied tasks.

Planning Summary

Game Developer Path

Best suited to a person who enjoys creative problem-solving, spatial thinking, programming, and long uninterrupted development sessions. The strongest portfolio is a public, playable game with version-controlled source code and documented gameplay.

Welder Backup Path

Best suited to a person who enjoys physical fabrication, precise procedures, safety, and focused solo work. The strongest preparation is hands-on experience, recognized certifications, and practical welding qualification testing.
